ℹ️ About This Application

Understanding drug safety signal detection and regulatory submission readiness

🔬 What is Drug Safety Signal Detection?

A drug safety signal is information suggesting a possible causal relationship between a medicinal product and an adverse event that is unknown or incompletely documented. Pharmacovigilance teams monitor adverse-event reports to detect such signals early.

🧮 What is PRR (Proportional Reporting Ratio)?

PRR is a disproportionality measure used in signal detection. It compares the proportion of a specific adverse event among reports for a target drug with the proportion among all other drugs. A PRR ≥ 2 (with sufficient reports) is a common informal threshold for further review.

PRR = [a / (a+b)] / [c / (c+d)]
a = target drug + target event
b = target drug + other events
c = other drugs + target event
d = other drugs + other events

⚠️ Why Do Signals Require Further Evaluation?

Statistical signals such as a high PRR do not establish causation. Signals can arise due to reporting bias, confounding, or chance. Any detected signal must be evaluated by qualified pharmacovigilance experts before regulatory action is taken.

📁 What is the CTD?

The Common Technical Document (ICH M4) is an internationally harmonised format for new drug applications submitted to regulatory authorities such as the FDA, EMA, and PMDA.

  • Module 1 — Administrative & Prescribing Information
  • Module 2 — CTD Summaries
  • Module 3 — Quality
  • Module 4 — Nonclinical Study Reports
  • Module 5 — Clinical Study Reports

✅ How Does Submission Readiness Checking Work?

The checker compares the sections present in a submitted dossier against the required CTD structure. Each section is marked Complete, Needs Review, or Missing. A completeness percentage is calculated per module and overall.

🧪 Mock Data in This Prototype

All adverse-event reports, drug data, PRR values, and CTD dossier information in this application are entirely synthetic and fictional. They are designed to demonstrate the application logic. No real patient, drug, or regulatory data is used.
